## Summary

* Every headline number I could recompute independently was reproduced: data facts, in-sample Markowitz, KKT multipliers (budget multiplier -0.017942, convention below), the Monte Carlo verdicts, all 30 backtest metric rows to 3e-14, net returns rebuilt from stored weights to 2e-15, the stress windows, and the game numbers I tried.

## BLOCKER

**B-1. `docs/limitations.md` line 239 says the portfolios' stock returns are price returns and that no method receives dividends.** The stock prices were downloaded with `auto_adjust=True` (`data/raw/*.json`, `src/data.py`), so they are dividend-adjusted; the same file says so correctly at lines 28-29, and the sentence at 239 contradicts itself ("total-return portfolios ... also price returns"). Consequence: the portfolios include dividends, the NIFTY index series does not, so every comparison against the index favours the portfolios (index Sharpe 0.314 is understated relative to the stocks). No number changes; the wording would be wrong in the report. Proposed fix: reword line 239 to match lines 28-29.

## MAJOR

The git history does not show that the hypotheses were committed before the results (A5).** `docs/hypotheses.md`, the config `montecarlo` section and the Monte Carlo result files first appear in the same commit (058b232, 22:11); `docs/backtest_spec.md`, the config `backtest` pre-specification and the backtest results first appear in ee4211b (23:42). The two rows for `src/*.py` in `qa_preregistration_timeline.csv` show the scaffold commit because of a rename-following quirk of the scan; ignore them.

**M-2. The cross-validated DR portfolios are mostly 1/N, and the rankings among them are fragile (E5, E8, E9).**
* DR-CVaR (CV) equals 1/N to 1e-9 at 106 of 115 rebalances (epsilon index 12, 0.1); its other 9 rebalances use index 7. Its maximum drawdown equals 1/N's (-36.3%) because the whole peak-to-trough episode (2020-01-17 to 2020-03-23) is traded with exact 1/N weights; the series are not copies (190 days differ, up to 2.2 percentage points). So this is real, but the notes' "about 38 effective positions" (an average) hides that the portfolio is 1/N most of the time.
* DR-MSD (CV) sits at the grid maximum at 82 of 115 rebalances (71%); the extended-grid series at 91 of 115 (79%) at its own maximum (10). The grid binds.
* Ranking by Sharpe: unchanged when the evaluation start is shifted by one or two rebalances. Changed when weights are applied one day late: regime-aware DR-CVaR (baseline top, 0.789) falls to 0.761, below 1/N (0.768); DR-MSD (CV) (0.726) rises to 0.740, above plug-in (0.739). The "regime-aware DR-CVaR has the highest Sharpe" statement is exploratory in the notes and should stay labelled so. Table: `qa_eval_sensitivity.csv`.

## Addendum

Written after the follow-up analysis in `scripts/run_qa2.py` (tables `outputs/tables/qa2_dr_vs_eqw_qa2.csv`, `qa2_decomposition_qa2.csv`, `qa2_cvar95_comparisons_qa2.csv`). It corrects the way the audit summarised the cross-validated portfolios.

**Are the cross-validated portfolios exactly 1/N or near 1/N?** For DR-CVaR (CV epsilon), "exactly 1/N" is right at 106 of the 115 rebalances (L1 distance to 1/N below 1e-6, epsilon 0.1) and "near 1/N" is wrong at the other 9 (2020-03-30 to 2020-12-02, epsilon 0.00215): there the portfolio is sharply concentrated (L1 distance 1.20 to 1.30, effective N 13.5 to 15.1). The earlier "about 38 effective positions" is an average of those two groups and describes neither. For DR-MSD (CV epsilon), neither phrase is right for most of the sample: exactly 1/N at 10 rebalances, within L1 0.05 at 19, and clearly different at 96 (mean L1 0.51, largest 1.45, effective N from 10.4 to 39.9); it sits at the grid maximum at 82. Counts below 1e-6, 1e-3 and 0.05: DR-CVaR 106, 106, 106; DR-MSD 10, 10, 19.

**Why the Sharpe ratio of DR-CVaR (CV) is 0.679 against 0.768 for 1/N.** The gap (-0.089) comes almost entirely from the 9 concentrated periods, which cover 189 of the 2,405 days: the mean daily return difference is -0.000874 there and -0.000001 in the 106 periods that equal 1/N. The 1/N return series with only the 9-period differences added has a Sharpe ratio of 0.680, with only the 106-period differences 0.767. Costs are a small part: the average turnover is 0.076 against 0.052 and the Sharpe gap is -0.087 before costs and -0.089 after, so costs explain about 0.002. This is a decomposition, not a test; the 9 periods all fall in 2020, so it describes one episode. For DR-MSD (CV), the gap is -0.042 (-0.032 before costs; turnover 0.177), spread over the 96 periods that differ from 1/N.

**CVaR95 comparisons (DR minus comparator, percentage points of daily loss, 95% block-bootstrap interval; negative means lower tail risk).** Against plug-in max-Sharpe: DR-MSD -0.180 [-0.298, -0.058]; DR-CVaR -0.166 [-0.298, -0.020]; regime-aware DR-MSD -0.168 [-0.284, -0.048]; regime-aware DR-CVaR -0.267 [-0.399, -0.133]. Against 1/N: DR-MSD -0.120 [-0.218, -0.038]; DR-CVaR -0.106 [-0.205, -0.029]; regime-aware DR-MSD -0.108 [-0.210, -0.019]; regime-aware DR-CVaR -0.207 [-0.392, -0.070]. All eight intervals exclude zero. Because DR-CVaR (CV) equals 1/N at most rebalances, its CVaR difference against 1/N comes from the same 9 periods.
